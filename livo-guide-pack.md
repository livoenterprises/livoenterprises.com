# Topic: Getting started

*Guide topic 1 · version 5 · updated 2026-11-09 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/getting-started.html*

Livo asks for nothing at first launch. Getting Started is six optional steps, in the order that gets your first capsule onto a computer.

Open it from the line under the Gallery's header, from [Organize › Getting Started](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/getting-started), or from Settings › About. Each row shows a tick when it is done; swipe a row left to skip it for now. The rows are the loop Livo is built around: bring photos in, say who is in them and what happened, make a Memory, seal it, and open it where no Livo exists.

### 1. Bring in your photos

Point Livo at your Camera Roll. Livo makes small copies and leaves your photos where they are; it never deletes or changes them. Livo asks for permission to your photos when the import starts. See [Bringing in your photos](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/bringing-in-your-photos.html).

### 2. Name the people

In the Gallery, open a Moment (one day's photos) and tap the **tag icon** in its header. Tag the Moment opens on **People**: tap who is in these photos, then **Apply**. Select the photos a person is not in and tap their minus chip to take them off. The arrows move to the next Moment. See [Tag the Moment](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/tag-the-moment.html) and [Naming the faces](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/naming-the-faces.html).

### 3. Name an event, add a line of story

Give the day a name ("Zoo trip", "Thanksgiving 2019") and, when you like, a sentence about it. The event and its story go into every photo of the Moment, into your capsules and into search. See [Events and the story](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/describing-a-moment.html).

### 4. Seal your first capsule

Make a Memory first: select photos in the Gallery and tap **Make a Memory**, or open a Moment and tap **Make a Memory of these**. Then go to **Capsules** (the shield on the bottom bar) and tap **Seal**: pick the Memories to put in, name the capsule, and seal it. Under **Keep a copy**, choose where copies go (a folder in Files or iCloud Drive). See [Time Capsules](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/time-capsules-and-the-vault.html).

### 5. Open it on your computer

Find the capsule's folder where you kept a copy, or share it to yourself, and open **START HERE - open with any web browser.html**: a page with the Memories, photos, people and stories. Open any photo in the folder and the names, story and place are written into the photo itself, where Apple Photos, Windows and other apps show them. This is the point of Livo: what you know about these photos leaves with them, in plain files, and will still read in thirty years.

### 6. Where things are

A short tour of Livo's three surfaces and the book. See [Where things are](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/where-things-are.html).

### Your name and your look

Two more things worth a minute, both in Settings: [your name](https://livoenterprises.com/go/settings/your-name), which is stamped on every capsule you seal and signs your stories (typing it also makes you a Person, so you can tag yourself), and [your look](https://livoenterprises.com/go/settings/appearance), one of five palettes, dark or light.

# Topic: Bringing in your photos

*Guide topic 3 · version 7 · updated 2026-11-13 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/bringing-in-your-photos.html*

Livo reads your Camera Roll and keeps a small copy of each photo. Your photos stay where they are, and Livo never deletes or changes them.

### Add from Camera Roll

[Open the Gallery](https://livoenterprises.com/go/gallery), tap the **≡** menu at the top right, then **Add Photos**, then **Add from Camera Roll**. Livo asks for permission to your Photos the first time, then brings in every photo and video, newest first, including photos that live only in iCloud. A big library takes a while; you can keep using Livo, and the progress shows under the Gallery header. [Show me the menu](https://livoenterprises.com/go/how/add-photos).

If you allowed Livo only some of your photos, it works with those and says so. Before it starts, Livo checks your free space ("Importing about 140 items needs about 2.3 GB"): it warns if the import would leave under 2 GB, and will not start below 1 GB. If space runs out part way, the import pauses and keeps everything it has brought in; start another import to carry on.

### What Livo keeps

For every photo: its date, place, size and a small copy (800 × 800) that Livo uses when Photos is slow, when a photo is only in iCloud, or when it is gone from this phone. Your photos themselves stay in your Camera Roll. Deleting a photo in Livo never deletes it in Photos.

### The badge on the ≡ menu

The **≡** menu carries a small red number when there is something new to bring in: photos added to your Camera Roll since the last import. A photo Livo itself put in your Camera Roll (a photo from a capsule you received, say) is not counted as new.

### Other ways photos arrive

A Time Capsule or a gift someone sent you puts its photos in your Camera Roll and in Livo in one step; see [Sending to someone](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/sending-to-someone.html). With [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html), photos can also come from OneDrive and from printed photos you scan with the camera. [Open Scan Prints](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/curator/sources/scan-prints) to scan a printed photo, [open OneDrive Accounts](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/curator/sources/accounts) to connect OneDrive and choose folders to add, or [open Where My Photos Are](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/curator/sources/media-sources) to see where each photo is kept. A friend's Family Share arrives through [Receive from Family](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/curator/family/receive).

### Cleaning up

At the end of every import Livo looks for the same photo brought in twice and keeps one record of it, with everything you had written on either copy. Two records are treated as the same photo only when the whole files match. Your photos are not touched; only Livo's own duplicate record goes. The message says "Livo also cleaned up its library" when that happened.

# Topic: Moments and the Gallery

*Guide topic 4 · version 5 · updated 2026-11-11 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/moments-and-the-gallery.html*

A Moment is one day's photos. The Gallery shows them one Moment at a time, so naming and sorting stay small.

### Choosing a Moment

Tap the calendar at the top left to see your Moments, newest first, and pick one. The ‹ and › arrows beside the date move to the earlier and the later Moment. Tap **All Moments** to see the whole library at once (a big library takes a few seconds; the card says so); picking a Moment turns it off again. When you leave the Gallery for another surface and come back, it is where you left it, filter and all.

### The Moment header

Beside the date are two small icons. The **tag** opens Tag the Moment, where you say who is in the photos, what the event was and where it happened. The **face** opens Name the Faces, which shows every face Livo found in the Moment so you can name them. See [Tag the Moment](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/tag-the-moment.html) and [Naming the faces](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/naming-the-faces.html). The Moment's menu also offers **Make a Memory of these**.

### Marked photos

Swipe a photo up to mark it (a thumbs up). The **N Marked** pill shows how many marked photos the library has; tap it to see only them. Marked photos are the set a new Memory is made from; see [Making a Memory](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-memory.html).

### Selecting photos

Tap a photo to select it; keep dragging to select more. A row appears under the header: **Select All**, **Clear**, **+ Event** (put the selection in an event), **Make a Memory** and **Share**. The bar at the bottom offers **Tag** (people, places and tags on all of them at once), **Info** (one photo), **Delete** (which asks first and moves the photos to the Trash), and **…** for the rest: Rotate, Set Date, Stitch for videos, Find Moment, and the Curator tools when Curator is on.

### Opening a photo

Tap a selected photo, or double-tap any photo, to see it full screen. Swipe between photos, pinch to zoom, pull down to close. A LIVE pill plays a Live Photo. The buttons under a photo include Info, Rotate, Edit Photo and, on a video, Edit Segments (Livo Plus); see [Editing photos and videos](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/editing-photos-and-videos.html). The tag icon at the top opens the Details strip for that one photo. If the full-size picture cannot be shown, the caption under it says why; see [When something looks wrong](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/when-something-looks-wrong.html).

### The badges on a photo

A small mark in the corner tells you what a photo is: video, LIVE, stitched, used in a Memory, or **Aa** when Livo has read text on it. Curator adds marks for a scanned print and a photo in a Bucket.

# Topic: Tag the Moment

*Guide topic 5 · version 1 · updated 2026-11-09 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/tag-the-moment.html*

One Moment, one fact at a time. Pick the people, tap Apply, and the whole day is named.

### Opening it

[Open the Gallery](https://livoenterprises.com/go/gallery), pick a Moment, and tap the **tag icon** beside the date. The screen shows the Moment's title, a count ("12 photos · Moment 3 of 40"), arrows for the previous and next Moment, and four tabs: **Event**, **Kind**, **People** and **Place**. Only the open tab's field shows, so the photos keep the room. A dot on a tab means you have picked something for it.

### People

Tap who is in these photos. The names you use most are at the top; **Likely** lists the people already in this event and these days, so one tap each is usually enough; the person icon adds someone new. Then tap **Apply (12)**. Apply adds and never removes: a photo that already had a name keeps it.

To take a person off the photos they are not in: select those photos (tap them in the strip or the big picture) and tap the person's **minus chip** under "Take off the 3 selected". One tap, and the name leaves those photos only. **Same people as the photo above** copies one photo's people to the next, for a run of photos of the same group.

### Event and Place

On **Event**, type a few letters to find any event you have made, or pick **Last used**; a new name makes a new event. An event's dates grow as you add photos to it, so one event can cover a whole trip. On **Place**, type a few letters or pick from your Places. Apply writes the event and the place onto the selected photos. See [Events and the story](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/describing-a-moment.html) for the story that goes with an event.

### Kind

A Kind says what a photo is rather than who is in it: a selfie or a screenshot, so the Filter can hide them. Livo marks the ones it can tell on its own; this tab is for the rest. [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html) adds more Kinds.

### The strip and the big photo

A strip of small thumbnails runs above one large photo. Swipe the big photo left and right to move through the day in time order, or tap a thumbnail to jump. Each thumbnail is badged for the open tab: a filled dot when the photo has that fact, a dim ring when it does not, so what is still missing shows at a glance. **Tap** the big photo to include or skip it for Apply; **press and hold** opens it full screen. Beside it are that one photo's own pills; **×** takes one off at once, and **Edit** opens the full tag screen for that photo (event, kind, people, place, story and tags).

### Undo

Every Apply and every take-off can be taken back. **Undo** at the top undoes the last one; tap it again for the one before, as many times as you like in this Moment. Nothing leaves the phone and nothing is guessed: a name Livo does not know becomes a dashed chip that asks you.

### Moving on

The arrows go to the previous and next Moment, and the header resets for each one. The count at the top tells you how much of the library has been looked at.

# Topic: Naming the faces

*Guide topic 6 · version 4 · updated 2026-11-10 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/naming-the-faces.html*

Livo finds the faces; you say who they are. It never guesses a name.

### Opening it

[Open the Gallery](https://livoenterprises.com/go/gallery), pick a Moment, and tap the **face icon** beside the date. Livo finds the faces in that Moment's photos (the first time takes a moment) and shows each one as a small chip, in the order the photos were taken.

### Name, then tap

1. Tap **Who is this?** and pick a person — type a name, say it, or choose from the list; **Add "…" as a new person** makes one on the spot. The band at the top of the list, **In this Moment, and recent**, holds the people already tagged on these photos and the people you have named in earlier Moments this session, so a name is one tap once you have used it.
2. Tap every chip that is that person. Chips from the same photo dim, because a person appears only once in a photo.
3. Tap **Apply**. Those chips leave the grid and the photos are tagged with that person.
4. Pick the next person. When everyone left is one person, **Everyone else is…** names them all at once.

**Press and hold a chip** to see the whole photo, large, with that face outlined, and a menu: **Name this face…**, **Not a face**, **Don't name** (a stranger), **Hide for now**, **Show the photo**. The chips are three across so each face is big enough to read. **Skip rest** marks everything left as Don't name, after asking, and goes on to the next Moment with faces to name. Faces you marked **Not a face** wait in a collapsed row at the bottom, "Not faces (3)"; open it and tap a chip, or its **Restore**, to put that face back as unnamed.

### Review

The **Review** switch at the top shows every named face in this Moment, grouped by name. Tap a face to rename it or un-name it. Previous and Next move through Moments that have named faces.

### Moving on

When a Moment is done, the card tells you how many Moments still have faces to name. **Next Moment** goes to the next one that does, and the Gallery behind moves with you. If photos in the Moment carry a person whose face is not among the named ones (you tagged the whole day generously in Tag the Moment, say), the card offers **Take them off** with the count and the names, and an Undo; it counts only photos where every face is named, never one with a face Livo found but you did not name.

### Where the names go

Naming a face tags the photo with that person, so Tag the Moment, the person's screen, the Filter and Livo Search all find it; Tag the Moment is the faster way when a Moment has no faces to find, or when you know who was there without looking. Removing a person from a photo also un-names their face there. No face measurements are stored and nothing leaves the phone; a Time Capsule carries the names, never the face boxes.

# Topic: Events and the story

*Guide topic 7 · version 3 · updated 2026-11-09 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/describing-a-moment.html*

A Moment is Livo's guess from the dates. An Event is what you say happened, and the story is how you would tell it.

### What an Event is

An Event has a name, the way you would say it ("Family beach trip 2024", "Emma's 5th birthday"), a first and last day, the people who were there, the places, and other names it goes by. One Event can cover many Moments, and two can overlap (a birthday in the middle of a vacation). A photo is in an Event only when you put it there; the dates alone never decide. Events live in [Organize › Events](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/events), in the Events row at the top of Memory Lane, in Tag the Moment and in search.

### Putting photos in an Event

Three ways, all the same list:

- **+ Event** on the Gallery's selection row, or on a Moment's header for the whole Moment. The list shows the past Events covering those dates, newest first, with **New event** at the top already filled with the photos' dates. Tap one and the photos are in it at once ("Added 12 to Zoo trip").
- **Tag the Moment › Event** tab: type a few letters to find any event, or pick **Last used**, then Apply. See [Tag the Moment](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/tag-the-moment.html).
- **Search**: when nothing matches what you typed, the card offers **Make it an event** with your words as its name.

An Event's dates grow as you add photos from other days, so one Event can follow a whole trip. Choosing an Event while tagging lists its people and places as **From the event** picks, unticked: you tick who is in the frame and which stop this was.

### The story

The story is a sentence or a paragraph, in your words, written onto the photos themselves and into every capsule and gift. Write it in three places:

- **On one photo**: open it full screen and tap the tag icon for the Details strip (People · Places · Tags · Story), or tap **Edit** beside the big photo in Tag the Moment. A photo in an Event that has no story yet is offered the Event's name as a start.
- **On several photos**: select them and tap **Tag** in the bottom bar. A story you type is added only to photos that have none; if some already have one, Livo asks whether to **Keep Theirs**, **Add Where Empty** or **Replace Theirs**. Nothing is written until you tap **Apply**.
- **On a Memory**: the canvas has its own story, read aloud if you record it; see [Making a Memory](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-memory.html).

**Show Names Instead of "I"** in Settings changes how a story reads on screen only; what you wrote is never rewritten.

### Tags

A tag is one word or two ("cabin", "first day of school") for things that are not a person, place or event. Add them on one photo's Details strip, or on a selection with **Tag**, where the tags the photos already have are listed, each with a minus that takes it off every selected photo. Livo never adds a tag on its own. The list of all your tags, with rename and merge, is a [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html) tool under Organize.

### Undo

Every save from these screens has an **Undo** that takes the last one back exactly, and in Tag the Moment you can undo as many steps as you like.

# Topic: Finding photos

*Guide topic 8 · version 3 · updated 2026-11-09 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/finding-photos.html*

Filter narrows the Gallery by what you pick. Livo Search finds photos from a few words, and shows what it understood.

### Filter

[Open the Filter](https://livoenterprises.com/go/gallery/filter) from the funnel at the top left of the Gallery. Pick people (any of them, or all of them together), places, events, tags, a year, month or day, and switches such as Marked Only, Faces Not All Named, No Faces, No Place, Photos or Videos, and Hide Selfies or Hide Screenshots. [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html) adds more: Hide Thumbs Down, From Family Only, Scanned Prints Only and the other Kinds. The filter keeps the Moment you are in and skips Moments with nothing in them; a banner at the top says what is on, and its ✕ clears it. The same filter also narrows Memory Lane.

### Livo Search

Tap **Search** in the bottom bar ([open Livo Search](https://livoenterprises.com/go/search)), or tap the search field at the top of the Gallery, and type or dictate a few words: a name, a place, a tag, a word from a story, a word from a scanned print, or a date. As you type, Livo suggests names, places, tags and words from your stories, and shows your recent searches. The results appear in the Gallery.

Under the field, Livo shows what it understood as **chips**, one for each piece: a person, a place, a tag, a story word, a date, or a kind such as videos or favorites. Tap a chip to see what else that word could mean, with the number of photos for each, and pick one. A gray chip is a word Livo could not place; a dotted chip is a spelling guess ("Anna?") you can confirm. Tap a chip's ✕ to drop it.

Livo understands ordinary phrasing:

- **Dates and ages:** "last summer", "Christmas 2019", "since 2020", "Anna at 16", "when Tom was born".
- **Family words:** "my daughters", "my son-in-law", "my daughter's birthday", from the relationships you set on People.
- **Only:** "only Jack", "just the cousins", "Jane and Jack alone". Only named people count.
- **Events:** the name of any event you made ("zoo trip", "Thanksgiving 2019") finds its photos; when an event has none yet, the card says so.
- **Kinds:** videos, only photos, marked, edited photos, edited segments, stitched videos, video frames.

If nothing is found, Livo suggests the nearest spelling or widens one piece and says which; it does not leave you at an empty screen.

Livo Search can learn, but only when you say yes. If you change a chip to a person, Livo may ask "Add 'Grandpa Joe' as a name for Joseph?" or "Remember 'the cabin' as Lake Lure house?". Photos you open and share lift their words higher in the suggestions, and never change what a search finds. See and remove everything Livo has learned in Settings › Livo Search › What Livo has learned. Livo Search only finds; it never changes anything.

While a search is on, the Filter sheet shows it under **From Livo Search**, with **Clear search** to drop just the search and keep your other filters.

### Finding one photo's Moment

With one photo selected, **Find Moment** jumps to the Moment it belongs to and scrolls to it. On the Memory canvas, tapping a photo does the same.

# Topic: Editing photos and videos

*Guide topic 9 · version 6 · updated 2026-11-11 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/editing-photos-and-videos.html*

Every edit in Livo makes a new photo or video next to the original. The original is never changed, and nothing is deleted.

### The one rule

Livo does not touch the files you brought in. Rotate is a note Livo keeps for itself. Edit Photo, Edit Segments, Save Frame and Stitch each make a **new** file, saved where the original lives (the Camera Roll, or with Curator the same OneDrive folder), and bring it into Livo with its own badge and its own line in the Filter. The original stays as it was. If you want it out of the way afterwards, a Bucket (a Curator tool) takes it off the Gallery without deleting it.

### Rotate

Select a photo and tap **Rotate** in the bottom bar, or use the Rotate button in the full-screen viewer. Livo turns the picture a quarter turn each tap and remembers it. The photo in Photos is not changed; whenever a copy leaves Livo (a Memory video, a capsule, a share), the turn is baked in.

### Edit Photo

Open a photo full screen and tap **Edit Photo**. Two tools: **Crop** (drag the corners or the box) and **Adjust** (Brightness and Contrast). **Save** writes a new photo, `Edited_` and the time, to the Camera Roll or the OneDrive folder; it shows in the Moment of the original's date with the edited badge. The Filter has **Edited Photos Only**.

### Edit Segments (Livo Plus)

Edit Segments is part of Livo Plus; without it the film button opens the Livo Plus screen, and segments you already cut stay in Livo. Edit Photo and Rotate are free.

For a long recording — a transferred videotape, an hour of a party — open the video full screen and tap the film button, **Edit Segments**. It is a mark-in, mark-out editor:

1. Find where a recording starts: drag the filmstrip, nudge a second at a time, or play, fast-forward and rewind and watch for the break.
2. Tap **Mark Segment Start**.
3. Set **Date/time on tape** — read it off the picture if the tape shows one, or your best memory.
4. Find where it ends and tap **Mark Segment End & Save**.

Livo saves that stretch as a new video named with the date you typed, so it lands in the Moment of that date, not the day the tape was transferred. The next segment starts where this one ended, so you work along the tape in order. Each segment carries the segment badge; the Filter has **Edited Segments Only**. The long original is untouched.

### Save a frame as a photo (Livo Plus)

On the same Edit Segments screen, stop on the picture you want — the face, the cake, the moment — and tap **Save Frame as Photo**. Livo takes that one frame at the video's full size and saves it as a new photo in your Camera Roll, dated the video's date plus the time into the video, so it sits right after the video in the same Moment. Nothing is copied from the video: the frame starts with no story or tags, and you add what it needs. The tile carries the frame badge; Info says which video it came from and where; the Filter has **Video Frames Only**. A frame is never sharper than the video it came from.

### Stitch (Livo Plus)

Select two or more videos in the Gallery and tap **Stitch** in the bottom bar. Livo lists the clips in the order they were taken, the total running time, and where the result will go (where the first clip lives). **Stitch** makes one new video of all of them, end to end. When the clips are all the same way up it is joined without re-encoding, so nothing is lost; otherwise it is re-encoded. **Stop** works at any time before saving starts: it cancels the join, removes any partial file and says "Stopped; nothing was saved." Clips with no date go last, and the result is dated by the earliest dated clip. The result has the stitched badge; the Filter has **Stitched Videos Only**. Edit Segments works on a stitched video like any other.

### What Livo does not do

Trim a video in place, change its speed, add titles or music, or edit sound. A Memory is the place to put photos and clips together with a story and your voice — see [Making a Memory](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-memory.html).

Three ways. Mark the photos you want (swipe up in the Gallery) and tap **New Memory** on the marked row. Or select photos and tap **Make a Memory** on the selection row. **Share › Share as Video** renders the Memory as a video: the hero with the title as the first frame, each photo in turn with the names of the people in it and the story, your narration over it. When you make a gift of a Memory, Livo makes this video for you; see [Making a gift](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-gift.html).

### Editing later

Every Memory stays editable: open it from Memory Lane, tap **Edit**. Editing never changes your marked photos. **Reorder Photos** puts them in any order you like.

[Open Capsules](https://livoenterprises.com/go/vault), the shield on the bottom bar, and tap **Seal**. The capsule's README lists anything not included.

### What is inside

The photos and videos at full quality, with the names, the place and the story written into each photo where Photos, Windows and other apps can read them; the story; your narration; the Memory as a video; the names of the people and places; a list of every file with its checksum; a README that says what each file is; and a page called **START HERE - open with any web browser.html**. Unzip the capsule on any computer and open that page: no app, no account, nothing to install. The README asks whoever keeps it to copy it to a new drive every five years or so.

### Keep a copy

Until you choose a place, a capsule is only on this phone, in the Files app under On My iPhone › Livo › TimeCapsules. Under **Keep a copy** on the Capsules screen, add a folder from the Files app (iCloud Drive, or another app's folder) and every capsule you seal is copied there too, and checked now and then. Each capsule shows its name, the date and time it was sealed, and its state: **Checked 6 Nov · 2 places** when all is well; **Not checked yet**; **Needs attention**; **Re-seal**; **Lost**. The line at the top sums them up. Tap a capsule for its screen: look inside, **Share with Livo**, **Keep a copy**, the checks and repairs, and Delete. Delete says which copies Livo knows of and offers **Delete and Remove Its Copies** or **Delete Here Only**.

### A gift's video and book

A capsule that was made as a gift has a section called **The gift** with **The video** and **The book (PDF)**; an older capsule with a video has **Inside**. Each pulls the file out of the capsule and opens the share sheet under a name a person would keep ("For Grandma - Birthday - the book.pdf"), so the capsule is where the video and the book live, and nothing is stored twice. **Give it again…** (Livo Plus) reopens the whole "Your gift is ready" screen for that gift, and **+ › Make a Gift…** at the top of Capsules starts a new one from any Memory. See [Making a gift](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-gift.html).

### Bringing one in

**Bring in** at the top takes a capsule file from Files. Its photos go into your Camera Roll and your Gallery, and the Memory appears in Memory Lane; see [Sending to someone](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/sending-to-someone.html).

### Older versions

Re-sealing a Memory makes a new capsule and keeps the old one under Older Versions; nothing you sealed is ever thrown away by Livo.

# Topic: Making a gift

*Guide topic 14 · version 2 · updated 2026-11-10 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/making-a-gift.html*

A gift is a Memory with a bow on it. Say who it is for, and Livo makes the video, seals the gift and lays out every way to give it.

### Who it is for

A gift is made for the person receiving it, who may be older, may be on an iPad or a Windows PC, and will not unzip anything. So every gift has three layers, from the least effort to the most: a **video** that plays with one tap; a **page** that opens in any web browser; and the **files** themselves, the photos with the names, story and place written into them, which is what makes it something to keep rather than a link that expires.

### Making one

Open a Memory and tap **Share › Make a Gift…**, press and hold a Memory's card in [Memory Lane](https://livoenterprises.com/go/memory-lane) and tap **Make a Gift…**, or in [Capsules](https://livoenterprises.com/go/vault) tap **+ › Make a Gift…** and choose the Memory.

1. **The dedication.** Who it is for, from (your name, already filled in), the occasion (Birthday, Anniversary, Holiday, Just because, In memory of) and a few words in your own. They become the first screen of the video, the top of the page inside the gift, and the first lines of its README, and the gift is named for them ("For Grandma - Birthday", or "In memory of Dad", which gets the quieter wording throughout). The sheet also says how much room the phone needs while the gift is made, and how much it has.
2. **Make the gift.** Livo fetches the photos at full size, makes the video (the dedication, then each photo with the names of the people in it and the story, your narration over it, and "Made by …" at the end, with a small "with Livo" line unless you have Livo Plus), and seals everything as a Time Capsule with the video first in the folder. A progress card shows each step, with **Stop**. Stopping keeps nothing, and the dedication stays on the Memory for next time. If the video could not be made, the gift is still complete without it, and Capsules can make the video later.
3. **Your gift is ready.** One screen with everything you can do with it.

### Your gift is ready

- **Send the video**: the video alone, by Messages or AirDrop, or saved to Files. The line under it says the size and whether it fits in Messages.
- **Give the whole gift**: the sealed capsule, by AirDrop, Messages, Mail or Files. Whoever receives it unzips it and opens START HERE; with Livo on an iPhone, tapping it brings the Memory in.
- **Save the photos for a frame or a TV**: the photos as plain JPEGs in a folder, for a digital frame's app, a USB stick or a TV.
- **Order prints**: print-size photos as plain JPEGs (free); and, with [Livo Plus](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/livo-and-livo-plus.html), the same with a strip under each that says who it is and when, the gift book (an 8 × 8 inch PDF with a page per photo and the names under each) and a 16 × 20 collage poster. Each opens the share sheet on the file or folder, ready for a photo kiosk, a print service's app, or your own printer. See [Order prints](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/order-prints.html).

Every export shows a progress bar and a **Stop** button while it runs; Stop leaves nothing behind. A copy of the gift is kept where your capsules go (see [Keeping capsules safe](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/keeping-capsules-safe.html)), and the gift's video and book can be pulled out again any time from its screen in Capsules; **Give it again** there (Livo Plus) reopens this whole screen.

### What the person receives

On an iPhone with Livo, the gift opens with its dedication and a **Watch** button; **Add to My Livo** brings the Memory in, and **Save to Photos as an album** keeps the photos and the video in Photos. On any computer, unzipping it and opening START HERE shows the dedication, plays the video, and lists the photos with their names and stories. A photo frame or a TV takes the folder of photos. See [Sending to someone](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/sending-to-someone.html).

### In memory of

Choosing **In memory of** changes the words everywhere: the title card, the page, the README and the gift's name, with nothing playful.

### The gift book

Also on Your gift is ready, with Livo Plus: **The gift book**, an 8 × 8 inch square PDF with the dedication on the cover, a page per photo with the names and the month under each, the story, and a back page listing everyone in the book. **A collage poster** (Livo Plus) lays the Memory's photos out as one 16 × 20 inch picture, for a frame or a print service's poster size.

### A book from any Memories and pages

The book is not stored in Livo; your Memories and pages are the source, and it is made again whenever you ask.

### Room and time

Making print files pulls the full-size photos first, over Wi-Fi when they are in iCloud, so a book of forty photos takes a minute or two. Every one of these jobs shows its progress and can be stopped, and stopping leaves nothing behind.

# Topic: Keeping capsules safe

*Guide topic 16 · version 3 · updated 2026-11-09 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/keeping-capsules-safe.html*

Two places at least, checked on a schedule, and you are told while it can still be fixed. That is the promise behind Capsules.

### Keep a copy

[Open Capsules › Keep a copy](https://livoenterprises.com/go/vault/places). **This phone** is always one place. Add up to five **folders of your own** from the Files app: iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, a drive plugged into the phone. Every capsule you seal is copied to each of them. A place that is not reachable right now (a drive unplugged, no network) is simply "pending"; the copy is written the next time Livo sees it. Removing a place deletes nothing. With [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html), your OneDrive's **Livo Vault** folder is a place as well, on by default when you are signed in.

Livo writes only its own capsule files into a place and never deletes anything there.

### Checking

Livo quick-checks each capsule's copies about once a week on its own, and while the app is closed it may check a few in the background. **Check** at the top of Capsules offers **Check All**. A capsule's own screen has Quick Check and Verify Every File, which reads each copy and proves it byte for byte.

You only hear about trouble: one quiet notification when a copy needs attention, never a message that everything is fine.

### Repair, Re-seal, Lost

If one copy is damaged or missing, the capsule's screen offers **Repair** from a copy that checked out, in either direction. Nothing is ever replaced except from a verified copy, and a damaged file is moved aside, not deleted. If no good copy is left but the Memory is still here, **Re-seal from the Memory** makes a fresh capsule. If the Memory is gone too, the capsule is **Lost**, and Livo says so plainly.

### Keepsakes

For a Memory that must never change, one you inherited, **Keep forever (a Keepsake)** on its screen, or when you receive it, marks it so that it is only ever repaired, never re-sealed or overwritten. With Curator, a Keepsake also gets a third copy in OneDrive › **Livo Keepsakes**, a folder Livo writes once and never deletes from.

# Topic: Backup & Restore

*Guide topic 17 · version 5 · updated 2026-11-12 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/backup-and-restore.html*

A backup holds everything Livo knows about your photos. Your photos themselves stay where they are.

### What is in a backup

[Open Organize › Curator › Backup & Restore](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/backup). A backup holds every name, place, story, tag and face you have added, your Memories and narrations, Livo's small copies of your photos, what Livo Search has learned, your family folders list, and your settings. It does not hold the photos themselves (they stay in Photos, or in OneDrive with Curator) or your Time Capsules (they are files; see [Keeping capsules safe](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/keeping-capsules-safe.html)).

### Making one

**Create Backup Now** runs in the background; the card and Activity show the steps. Livo keeps the screen on while it works. Backups are named Livo_Backup_ with the date and time, and nothing already there is ever overwritten. The backup is saved on this phone (On My iPhone › Livo › Livo Backup); **Share** on a backup puts it in iCloud Drive or any folder in Files. With [Curator](https://livoenterprises.com/guide/curator.html), **Copy new backups to OneDrive** copies each one to the private **Livo Backup** folder in your OneDrive as well. "Last backup: …" under the row tells you how long ago. Livo never backs up on its own and never reminds you with a pop-up. [Show me Create Backup Now](https://livoenterprises.com/go/how/backup-now) or [Restore from a Different Location](https://livoenterprises.com/go/how/restore-backup).

A backup on the phone is deleted with the app, so keep one off the phone: share it to iCloud Drive, or, with Curator, let Livo copy it to OneDrive.

### Restoring

Tap a backup in the list (on the phone, or from Files, or with Curator under **Backups in OneDrive**) and **Restore**. Restoring replaces what Livo has now (your photos' records, people, places, tags, stories, Memories and learned search data) with what the backup holds; this cannot be undone. It does not touch your Time Capsule files, your Camera Roll, or anything in a cloud account. Swiping a backup on the phone to delete it asks first; a copy in OneDrive stays. On the same phone, every photo links back at once.

### A new phone

Install Livo, allow access to Photos, and restore the backup. Livo finds each photo in the new phone's library by its iCloud identity, then by its content, and re-links it; a photo it cannot find keeps its record and small copy, and is counted ("N photos not found on this phone"). Copy your Time Capsules from the folder where you keep a copy (iCloud Drive, or the old phone's TimeCapsules folder). With Curator, sign in to OneDrive again for OneDrive photos and the Livo Vault folder.

### Check a backup drive (Curator)

A copy of your photos on an external drive is only a backup if it is complete. Under **Check a backup drive**, **Write the check kit** makes two plain files from what Livo knows about every photo (name, size, a fingerprint of its first 64 KB, date): a manifest and a page called **CHECK MY BACKUP - open with Chrome or Edge**. **Write it to OneDrive** puts them in a folder called Livo Archive Check at the top of each signed-in OneDrive, so the next mirror carries them to the drive; **Share the kit…** hands them to AirDrop or Files instead. On the Mac or PC, open the page *from the drive*, tap **Choose the folder**, pick the mirrored folder, and it reports expected, found, match, missing and changed, with the names; **Check fingerprints too** reads the start of each file to confirm it is the same file, and finds photos saved under another name. Nothing is sent anywhere. Before you mirror, mark the OneDrive photo folders **Always keep on this device**, or the mirror copies placeholders. A copy in a cloud account lasts only as long as the account; keep one on a drive you own.

# Topic: Trash, and what Livo never deletes

*Guide topic 18 · version 4 · updated 2026-11-11 · https://livoenterprises.com/guide/trash.html*

Deleting in Livo is two steps, and the second always says exactly what it does.

### Two steps

Deleting a photo, Memory, capsule, person or place moves it to the Trash ([Organize › Trash](https://livoenterprises.com/go/organize/trash)), where it waits until you **Restore** it or **Empty Trash** (or **Delete Forever** on one item). The Trash never empties itself.

### What Empty Trash removes

- A **photo**: Livo's record and its small copy. The photo in Photos (or OneDrive) is untouched, and Livo remembers not to import it again.
- A **Memory**: the story, order and narration.
- A **Time Capsule**: its record and its file on this phone (a copy you kept in a folder is removed only if you choose **Delete and Remove Its Copies**; a Keepsake's copy is never removed).
- A **Bucket** (Curator): only the empty Bucket itself. Deleting a Bucket first sends its photos back to the Gallery.
- A **person** or **place**: the entry itself. Restoring a merged person or place undoes the merge.

The confirmation names the consequence for that kind of thing.

### What Livo never deletes

Your photos and videos in Photos. Anything in iCloud Drive or OneDrive outside Livo's own folders, or in another person's account. Anything in a Keep-a-copy folder you chose, other than the capsule copies Livo itself wrote there. A Keepsake file. A capsule file you opened from the Files app. Nothing is ever deleted on its own, on a schedule, or because space is low.

# Privacy Policy

*Version 5 · updated 2026-11-10*

Livo keeps your photos, stories and recordings on your phone and in storage you choose. Livo Enterprises does not collect them, does not see them, and has no servers that could.

This policy applies to Livo, sold on the App Store as LivoCapsule.

### What Livo keeps, and where

Everything Livo makes is stored on your iPhone or iPad: the names, places and stories you add to your photos, the Memories you assemble, your narration recordings, the Time Capsules you seal, the text Livo reads from scanned prints, what Livo Search has learned from you (the meanings you confirmed and your recent searches), and your settings. A count of the free Memories you have created is also kept in your iOS keychain, so that reinstalling the app does not reset it. Your photos and videos themselves stay in your Camera Roll or your OneDrive; Livo keeps a small copy of each photo so that it can still show you one that is no longer in Photos.

Time Capsules and backups are ordinary files. Capsules are kept in the Files app, under On My iPhone › Livo › TimeCapsules, so you can copy them wherever you like. None of this is sent to Livo Enterprises.

### Permissions the app asks for

Each permission is asked for the first time a feature needs it, and each can be withdrawn at any time in iOS Settings. Livo keeps working without it, minus that feature.

- **Photos.** To show your photo library, bring photos into Livo, and save new photos (edited copies, Memory videos, scanned prints, photos received from family) to it. If you allow only some photos, Livo works with those. Livo never deletes or changes an original.
- **Camera.** Only for Scan Prints, which photographs printed pictures one at a time with Apple's document scanner. The scans are saved to your Camera Roll.
- **Microphone.** Only when you tap record, to capture a narration. The recording is saved on your device with the Memory it belongs to.
- **Location.** Livo reads the location already inside your photos to suggest place names. It never tracks where your device is.
- **Notifications.** Livo may tell you, quietly, when a family member has shared something or when a Time Capsule's stored copy needs attention. Notifications are made on your phone; nothing is sent through a server. You can turn them off in iOS Settings.
- **Background App Refresh.** When allowed, Livo occasionally checks your family's shared folders and your Time Capsules' stored copies while it is closed. It downloads nothing in the background.

Reading the text in a scanned print, and finding faces so you can name them, happen on your device with Apple's own frameworks. Livo never guesses who a person is; every name is one you typed or tapped.

### Microsoft OneDrive (optional)

If you choose to sign in to a Microsoft account, Livo uses Microsoft's own sign-in. Your Microsoft password is never seen by the app; a sign-in token is kept in the device's secure keychain, and signing out removes it. Requests go from your device to Microsoft and nowhere else. Microsoft's handling of your account is governed by [Microsoft's privacy statement](https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement).

With OneDrive, Livo can:

- read photos and videos from the folders you pick;
- keep a copy of your Livo backups in a folder named Livo Backup, which only you can see;

- share Memory videos, photos and capsules with your family through a folder named Livo Family Share, which Livo shares with the family members you choose, as view-only, and read what your family has shared in theirs.

Livo writes only inside those folders in your own OneDrive, deletes there only what you ask it to delete, and never writes in another person's drive.

### Other places you choose

You can ask Livo to keep copies of your Time Capsules in other folders you pick in the Files app, such as iCloud Drive or another cloud service. Livo writes only its own capsule files there and never deletes anything in those folders.

# Glossary

- **Moment:** a day's photos grouped together in the Gallery, newest first. Tag the Moment says who is in it and what happened.
- **Marked:** a photo you swiped up on (a thumbs up). Marked photos are the set a new Memory is made from.
- **Memory:** a set of photos in order, a story, and your voice if you record one. Memories live in Memory Lane.
- **Time Capsule (capsule):** one or more Memories sealed into a set of files you can hand on. Capsules live under the Capsules surface.
- **Event:** something that happened, such as "Family beach trip 2024", covering one or more Moments. **Life Event:** a dated fact about a person, such as a birth or a wedding.
- **Kind:** what a photo is rather than who is in it, such as a selfie or a screenshot, so the Filter can hide them.
- **Tags:** words on photos that you can search and filter by, renamed or merged in Organize › Tags.
- **Bucket (Curator):** a hidden shelf. Photos moved there leave the Gallery, search and exports until you move them back. Nothing is deleted.
- **Trash:** where deleted photos, Memories, capsules, people and places wait until you restore them or empty it. It never empties itself.
- **Curator:** the Livo Plus tools for the one person who keeps the family's archive.
- **Livo Plus:** one purchase, made once and shared with your Apple family, never a subscription.
- **Family Share and the Family Library:** how each person in the family shares photos from their own OneDrive and receives what others share. Curator tools.
- **Activity:** the list of what Livo is doing in the background, with Pause and Stop.

### Note: Six terabytes, one family: how I actually use the family OneDrive

### Six terabytes, one family: how I actually use the family OneDrive

I keep my family’s photos. Not by appointment; it just happened, the way it happens in most families. I’m the one people send pictures to, the one with the scanner, the one who knows which hard drive the 2009 vacation is on. Thirty years of this leaves you with photos in every format a camera has ever produced, on every kind of storage anyone has ever sold, and a quiet dread that one bad drive or one closed account will take a decade with it.

The best storage deal I have found for this is not a photo service. It’s the Microsoft 365 Family plan: six people, one terabyte of OneDrive each, about $150 a year for all six. Six terabytes is more than a family of six will fill with photos in a lifetime. The trick is using it so the space helps instead of becoming one more place things get lost. This is how I do it.

#### Each person owns their own terabyte

The first decision is the one most families get wrong. It’s tempting to make one giant shared folder and have everyone dump into it. Don’t. On OneDrive, a file counts against the quota of the person who owns the folder it sits in, and anyone with edit rights to a shared folder can delete anything in it. One shared bucket means one person pays for everyone’s storage and one child’s slip takes out a sibling’s photos.

So every person in the family keeps their own photos in their own account. Each phone backs up its own Camera Roll into its own OneDrive with the OneDrive app’s camera upload. Nothing crosses between accounts unless someone decides it should. If a phone is lost, the photos are already in that person’s terabyte. If someone leaves the plan one day, their photos go with them, intact, because they were always theirs.

#### Sharing is a folder you write and others read

Within that, each person has one folder, shared with the other five, that works as an outbox. I put something in mine; the family can see it. I can’t put anything in theirs, and they can’t touch mine. The rights are view only, on purpose. The files stay in the owner’s quota, nobody can overwrite or delete anyone else’s, and there is no single folder whose permissions have to be right for the whole family’s archive to be safe.

This sounds restrictive and feels the opposite. Because nobody can damage anything, nobody is afraid to use it.

There are two kinds of shared folder, and it helps to keep them apart in your head.

The first is the shared folder: the outbox, for things meant to be seen this week. The video of the birthday Memory, so everyone can watch it on whatever they own. The capsule for Grandma’s eightieth. The twenty best photos from the trip, not the four hundred. The scan of the wedding photo somebody asked for at Thanksgiving. A short clip of the grandchildren for the aunt who isn’t on anything. Things go in, get looked at, and can stay or go; nobody treats it as the archive.

The second is the family library: a whole collection, shared once, also mine and read-only, that carries a small catalog of what’s in it so the family’s phones can find a photo by name instead of browsing thousands of files. That is where the long-lived sets live. The 1970s slides after they were scanned and named. Every photo of my parents I have been able to find, in one place. The complete run of a trip, not just the picks. The Christmas and Thanksgiving photos going back twenty years, by year. My father’s slides, which my sister also wants, without either of us emailing anything. A family member points their phone at it once, and from then on “Dad in 1978” finds the slide.

In LivoCapsule those two are Family Share and the Family Library: the first for the things you make, the second for the archive itself. The phone shows them the same way, as photos with names; the difference is only how long they are meant to stay and how big they are allowed to get.

#### The originals live in the cloud, not on the phone

A phone is for taking photos and looking at them, not for storing thirty years of them. Once the Camera Roll is safely in OneDrive, the originals’ home is OneDrive. The phone keeps small copies of everything, enough to scroll, search and tag without a connection, and fetches a full-size original only when one is needed: to zoom in, to seal a capsule, to make a gift. My own OneDrive holds about fifty thousand photos and videos, and all of them are on my phone at thumbnail size, a few gigabytes in all. The originals would not fit on any phone I could buy, and they don’t need to.

That also means the phone can be replaced without ceremony. A new one gets the small copies back and knows where every original is.

#### The cloud is a rented shelf

Here is the part nobody puts on the box. If the Microsoft 365 subscription lapses, the account drops back to the free 5 GB, and a terabyte of photos is suddenly two hundred times over the limit. Microsoft’s own rules for an over-limit OneDrive: everything goes read-only at once (you can still download and delete, but nothing new goes up); after three months over the limit the account is frozen; after six months Microsoft may delete the OneDrive and everything in it, and that deletion is not recoverable. Renewing stops the clock at any point. So a family that lets the plan go has a window of a few months to pull a terabyte down over a home connection before it is at risk. The cloud copy is not the archive. It is a rented shelf, and the rent is $150 a year.

Which is why the originals’ home has to be two places, and one of them has to be something you own outright: an external hard drive on a shelf. The way to keep it current is the OneDrive folder on a computer. Every Mac and PC with the OneDrive app has one, and it can be mirrored to a drive. Two things to know before you trust it:

- The OneDrive folder on a computer is mostly placeholders unless you tell it otherwise. Right-click the photo folders and choose Always keep on this device first; otherwise your “mirror” is a folder of icons. Give it a day to fill.
- Then mirror the folder to the drive and repeat it once a month. On a Mac, Carbon Copy Cloner or ChronoSync does it, or a plain Finder copy for the first time; on Windows, FreeFileSync, or robocopy /MIR for anyone comfortable with a command line.

The trouble with every backup is that nobody looks at it until the day they need it. So LivoCapsule can write two small files into the top of your OneDrive, a manifest of everything it knows about your photos and a page called CHECK MY BACKUP. They ride along with the next mirror. On the computer, open the page from the drive itself in Chrome or Edge, choose the folder, and it reads what is actually there and tells you: 48,912 expected, 48,912 found, 48,909 match, 2 missing, 1 changed, with the names. Nothing is sent anywhere; the page only reads the folder you point it at. A number you can read once a month is what confidence in a backup actually looks like.

#### One clean account, eventually

I’ll be honest about the mess. I have several Microsoft accounts from different eras, and photos ended up in most of them. Folders got named by whoever was holding the camera. Some of it was in a work account’s cloud for a while. My rule now is that everything gets found and named first, wherever it is, and consolidated second, into one clean account, one folder tree, one naming scheme. Naming is the part that can’t wait, because the people who know the faces in the oldest pictures are the people who won’t be here longest. Moving files can happen any time.

#### What goes in the shared folder, and what doesn’t

Shared: things made to be seen. Memory videos, because anyone can play an .mp4 with or without any particular app. Capsules, because they open in a web browser and carry the names and stories inside the photos. Picks: the photos worth the family’s time, not the four hundred near-duplicates of the cake.

Not shared: backups. A backup of a photo library’s names, stories and settings is private, and it lives in a private folder in the owner’s own account, never in anything the family can see. The same goes for the drafts, the duplicates, the photos that didn’t come out. The family folder is the album on the coffee table, not the drawer under it.

#### What I’d tell a family starting today

Get the plan, give everyone their own account, turn on camera upload on every phone, and stop there for a month. You have already solved the problem that matters most: nothing is only on a phone any more. Then make one shared, read-only folder each, and start putting the good things in it. Then, and only then, start on the shoebox.

Everything above works with the OneDrive app alone, plus one drive and one afternoon a month. It’s the structure I built LivoCapsule’s family features around, and it’s why LivoCapsule never needed a server: the family already has six terabytes, owned by the people whose photos they are. The tool’s job is to make the folders, set the rights, put the names and stories into the files, and hand you a way to check the copy on the shelf, so that what we know about these pictures is still there when the subscription, the phone and the app are long gone.

### Note: The thread I didn’t see until I wrote it down

### The thread I didn’t see until I wrote it down

For most of my working life I’ve held two beliefs that I thought were separate.

The first: people should own their data. Not license it, not lend it to a platform in exchange for convenience — own it, the way you own a photograph in a box. You decide who sees it. You decide whether it survives.

The second: devices should work for people, not the other way around. We’ve spent decades training humans to accommodate machines — learn the menu, learn the gesture, learn the file format, learn the language. Every one of those is the machine winning. The right direction is the opposite: you say what you want, in the words you already think in, and the machine figures out the rest.

I recently built an app and sat down to write about it, and realized these were never two beliefs. They’re one, and it’s about autonomy. Owning your data is autonomy over the past — what you’ve made and what you keep. Devices that work for you is autonomy over the present — how you spend your attention and what you’re forced to learn. Both are the same refusal: I will not rearrange my life around a system that should be serving me.

The app is called LivoCapsule. It seals family memories — photos, video, your own voice telling the story — into plain files that open anywhere, forever, with no account and no server. That’s the first belief, made into a thing you can hold.

But the part I didn’t expect is that I built it without learning the programming language. I described what I wanted, in English, to generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) tools, and held them to the same engineering discipline I’ve been using for years — requirements, design, verification, change control. That’s the second belief, applied to the building rather than the using. For the first time the tools met me in my own language, on my own process.

And then a third instance of the thread, which I only noticed writing this: the project itself. Every decision, every requirement, every session’s state lives in plain-text documents on my own disk — not in any AI’s memory. I can switch models tomorrow and lose nothing. The project is mine the same way the capsules are mine.

I don’t think I’m unusual in holding these beliefs. I think a lot of people hold them and have simply accepted that the technology won’t allow it. What I want to say is: the tools have caught up. Not everywhere, not completely, but enough that the thing you’ve been wanting — to be served rather than to serve — is finally available to someone willing to insist on it.

Insist on it.
